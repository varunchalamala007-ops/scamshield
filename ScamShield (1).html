What to do

    Two models, one answer

    1. A Naive Bayes classifier, trained in your browser. When the page loads it learns from labeled scam and normal messages, counting how often each word appears in each class with Laplace smoothing. For any new message it adds up log-odds per word. Words that push the total toward "scam" are the ones highlighted in red, so the model explains itself.

    2. A tactic detector. Pattern rules look for the six pressure tactics scammers rely on: urgency, money or gift cards, impersonating a trusted organization, secrecy, links, and requests for codes or passwords.

    Final score = 60% classifier probability + 40% tactic evidence. Blending keeps the score sensible even for wording the small training set has not seen.

    Why local? People who get scam messages are often older or less tech-confident, and the messages contain private details. Running offline means no account, no cost, and no data leaves the device.

    Limits: the demo is trained on a small hand-written set. Next steps are a larger multilingual dataset, a browser extension, and a family-sharing mode that alerts a trusted contact.

    **Project title:** ScamShield

**Problem statement:** Scam texts and emails cost people billions each year. Older adults, new immigrants, and first-time phone users are hit hardest, and most free tools just say "spam" without explaining why, so people do not learn to spot the next one.

**Solution overview:** ScamShield is a private, in-browser scam message checker. The user pastes a suspicious message and gets a risk score, the exact words that triggered it, the pressure tactics being used, and clear next steps.

**Key features:**
- Naive Bayes text classifier trained on-device at page load
- Word-level highlighting of the evidence behind the score
- Detection of six manipulation tactics: urgency, money/gift cards, impersonation, secrecy, suspicious links, credential requests
- Plain-language advice matched to the tactics found
- 100% offline: no uploads, no accounts, no cost

**Technologies used:** JavaScript, a from-scratch Naive Bayes implementation with Laplace smoothing, regex-based feature rules, HTML/CSS.

**Target users:** Older adults, families who want to protect them, and anyone who gets unexpected texts, emails, or calls asking for money or codes.

**Impact:** Teaches people why a message is risky, so the skill carries over to new scams. Works on any device with a browser, including low-cost phones.

**Team:** Solo submission.
